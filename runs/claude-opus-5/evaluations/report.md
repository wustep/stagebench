### Phase 1: Complete surface and basic piano

- Application libraries: `@vitejs/plugin-react` ^6.0.2, `react` ^19.2.7, `react-dom` ^19.2.7, `typescript` ~6.0.2, `vite` ^8.1.0

- Audio strategy: Live Web Audio synthesis. There are no recorded samples and no audio files of any kind in this artifact. Each note builds a voice from three OscillatorNodes (a sine fundamental plus two slightly inharmonic partials at 2.0027x and 3.0142x, each with its own exponential decay), summed into a per-voice GainNode set from velocity, through a per-voice lowpass BiquadFilterNode whose cutoff tracks pitch and velocity, into a shared piano-bus GainNode and then a master GainNode connected to the destination. A short hammer transient is played from one AudioBuffer of deterministic pseudo-random noise generated at engine construction time. Nothing bypasses the master gain.
- Generated sound sources: Piano partials (fundamental plus two inharmonic partials) — An honest approximation of a struck string: partial ratios are stretched slightly to model inharmonicity, upper partials decay faster than the fundamental, harder strikes put more energy into the upper partials and open the filter further, and low notes ring longer than high ones. It is not derived from, modelled on, or measured against any specific instrument or recording.; Hammer strike transient — A 90ms mono buffer of seeded pseudo-random noise (linear congruential generator, fixed seed 0x5eed1234) under a cubic decay envelope, shared by every voice. Deterministic: identical on every load. This is a generated buffer, not a recording of a piano hammer.
- Recorded sample provenance: No recorded or external sample sources declared
- Bundled audio files: None detected
- Audio note: No recorded sample sets are used or claimed in Phase 1, and no audio asset files ship in the artifact. The piano spec lets Phase 1 ship either a declared bundled sample set or honestly described synthesis; this artifact ships synthesis. Recorded, bundled, redistributable sample sets for Grand, Upright and Electric are Phase 2 scope and are deliberately absent here.
- Audio note: Only the keybed and the sustain input produce sound. All 146 controls on the six panel sections are presentation state only and are connected to no audio node whatsoever. This is asserted, not merely stated: a test operates every control in the inventory and shows the rendered destination output stays at digital silence and the audio graph node count does not change.
- Audio note: Audio, MIDI and timing are injectable boundaries (src/audio/graph.ts, src/audio/host.ts, src/input/midi.ts). Tests substitute a deterministic offline Web Audio implementation (src/audio/offline.ts) that really evaluates oscillators, buffer playback, parameter automation and the biquad filter sample by sample, so audio claims are asserted on rendered signals rather than on mocks. jsdom ships no Web Audio at all, so that renderer — not a browser audio engine — is what produces the sample data under test.
- Audio note: Reported audio states are only the ones that can really occur: unsupported (no Web Audio in the browser), idle (context suspended pending a user gesture), starting, ready, error. There is no asset download in Phase 1, so no loading progress is invented.
- Audio note: Polyphony is capped at 32 voices with deterministic stealing: the longest-running releasing voice first, then the longest-running sustained voice, then the longest-running sounding voice.

### Phase 3: Complete Stage 4 system

- Application libraries: `@vitejs/plugin-react` ^6.0.2, `react` ^19.2.7, `react-dom` ^19.2.7, `typescript` ~6.0.2, `vite` ^8.1.0

- Audio strategy: One AudioContext, one destination connection, for the whole instrument. Seven playable layers — Organ A/B, Piano A/B, Synth A/B/C — each build voices into a layer voice bus that runs through the documented six-unit chain (Mod 1 -> Mod 2 -> Delay -> Amp Sim/EQ -> Compressor -> Reverb) and a level fader, then into that section's bus or into the one shared Rotary Speaker. The three section buses and the rotary all meet at the master gain, which feeds a wave-shaper limiter and then the single destination; nothing bypasses that path. Piano: Grand, Upright and Electric play bundled recordings (71 mono 16-bit PCM WAV files, 24 kHz, 9.26 MB, under public/samples/); Clav, Digital and Misc are live oscillator synthesis, as is the labelled fallback voice. Organ: live additive synthesis — one oscillator per drawbar the model reads, plus generated noise-buffer transients for key click and pipe chiff, a delay-line vibrato/chorus stage, and one shared chain for both layers. Synth: live synthesis — oscillator stacks (single oscillator, sawtooth-through-shaper pulses, looped generated noise, looped generated single-cycle sync wavetables, detuned stacks, and a 2-operator FM pair), a biquad filter stack with an optional wave-shaper drive, three envelopes, an LFO and a vibrato LFO per layer, and an independent chain per layer. Organ and Synth layers are built the first time their section is switched on, so a piano-only program costs exactly the nodes Phase 2 cost. A keyboard-zone gain per note routes splits and applies crossfades before the voice is built.
- Generated sound sources: Synthesised piano voices (Clav, Digital, Misc, and the asset-failure fallback) — Honest approximations built from oscillators: a plucked wiry stack for Clav, an FM-style bell stack for Digital, a fourth-harmonic mallet stack for Misc, and the Phase 1 three-partial piano approximation as the fallback. None of these is derived from, modelled on, or measured against any specific instrument or recording, and none is described anywhere in the UI as a recording.; Hammer strike transient — A 90 ms mono buffer of seeded pseudo-random noise (linear congruential generator, fixed seed 0x5eed1234) under a cubic decay envelope, shared by every synthesised voice. Deterministic: identical on every load. This is a generated buffer, not a recording of a piano hammer. Sample-playing voices do not use it.; Effect wave-shaper curves — Computed tanh saturation curves for the amp models and the rotary drive, an absolute-value rectifier for the A-Wah and compressor envelope followers, a gain-reduction curve for the compressor, and a soft-knee curve for the master limiter. No audio content, just transfer functions.; Organ tone generation (B3, B3 Bass, Vox, Farf, Pipe 1, Pipe 2) — Four genuinely different generators, not one oscillator renamed: B3 is nine pure sines on the printed tonewheel footages with fixed per-wheel detune and two quiet mistuned neighbours as tonewheel leakage; Vox is triangle flutes plus sawtooth mixtures and a filtered/unfiltered mix drawbar pair; Farf is fixed-level square and sawtooth registers that switch on past half travel; Pipe 1 is detuned sine ranks with a slow attack and a noise chiff. B3 Bass reuses the B3 engine limited to the 16 foot and 8 foot drawbars, and Pipe 2 reuses Pipe 1 with a brighter principal registration — both are documented reuse models in the organ spec. Nothing here is derived from, modelled on, or measured against any recording of any organ, and nothing in the UI calls it a recording.; Synth oscillator sources (Pure, Sync, Multi, Super, FM-H) — Declared approximation: hard sync cannot be built from an OscillatorNode because there is no phase reset, so Sync Saw and Sync Square are naive, deliberately non-band-limited single-cycle wavetables generated at note-on and looped, which alias at the top of the keybed. The pulse waveforms are a sawtooth through a thresholding transfer curve with the DC component removed. All generated buffers (noise, sample-and-hold, wavetables) come from a seeded linear congruential generator or from closed-form arithmetic, so two renders of the same patch are identical. None of this is a recording and none of it is described as one.; Synth LFO Sample & Hold and vibrato buffers — A one-second stepped pseudo-random buffer, seeded (0x5f3a), looped as the Sample & Hold LFO shape, and a half-second seeded noise buffer for the White Noise waveform. Deterministic on every load; generated, not recorded.
