### Phase 1: Complete surface and basic piano

- Audio strategy: Live synthesis only. One piano-like voice is computed sample by sample by a TypeScript DSP kernel (additive synthesis: a bank of exponentially decaying sine partials per note). The kernel runs in an AudioWorklet; if the worklet cannot load, the same kernel runs in a ScriptProcessorNode and the status reads 'Fallback'. No recorded samples are bundled, fetched, or played, and no audio buffers are generated or stored.
- Generated sound sources: Basic Piano partial bank — Per note: up to 32 sine partials at stretched (inharmonic) frequencies, amplitudes shaped by strike position and a velocity-dependent spectral slope and felt lowpass, each decaying exponentially with higher partials decaying faster; the lowest 6 partials also carry a slower, 0.9-cent-sharp aftersound component for two-stage decay and beating. Attack ramp of 1-4 ms, pitch-dependent damper release, mild note-dependent stereo pan.; Hammer noise burst — A 12 ms decaying burst of lowpassed pseudo-random noise per strike, generated per sample by a xorshift32 generator seeded from the note number and strike order (deterministic). It is computed live; it is not a noise buffer.
- Recorded sample provenance: No recorded or external sample sources declared
- Bundled audio: None detected
- Audio note: There are zero audio files in this candidate: no .wav, .mp3, .ogg, .flac, or encoded sample data of any kind.
- Audio note: The voice is a synthesized approximation of a piano. It is described as synthesis in the UI ('synthesized piano voice (live additive synthesis, not a recording)') and is never presented as a recording or as a Nord piano model.
- Audio note: Recorded Grand / Upright / Electric sample sets required by specs/nord-stage-4.piano.json are Phase 2 scope and are not present.
- Audio note: Signal path: voices -> sum -> master gain (fixed 0.7 in Phase 1) -> soft limiter -> one output node -> AudioContext.destination. There is exactly one connection to the destination and nothing bypasses the master stage.
- Audio note: Polyphony is 32 voices with deterministic stealing: released voices first, then pedal-held, then held keys; oldest strike first within a class.
- Audio note: The panel's Master Level knob is decorative in Phase 1 and is not connected to the master gain.
