Notes
  • No sample sets are bundled in Phase 1. Grand, Upright and Electric recorded sample sets are Phase 2 scope; the piano spec allows honest synthesis for Phase 1.
  • No generated buffer is described as a recording. Nothing in the bundle is recorded audio.
  • The Web Audio path is: each voice -> voice low-pass -> master gain (0.3) -> AudioContext destination. No bypass.
  • The AudioContext is created on the first user gesture. If Web Audio is missing the instrument reports a labelled silent fallback; if the context refuses to resume it reports an error. The status line in the page shows which applies.
  • Pointer and computer-keyboard velocity is fixed at 100 in Phase 1 (KB Touch is Phase 2). MIDI uses the incoming note velocity.
  • Sustain applies to every voice in Phase 1 because there are no layers yet. Per-layer SUSTPED routing is Phase 2.
  • Known audio gaps: no pedal noise, no string resonance, no unison or soft-release shaping, no timbre filtering, no layer behaviour. All panel controls in the piano, effects, organ, synth and program sections are decorative in Phase 1.
Phase 2

Piano library and working effects

Audio strategy
Phase 2 has three recorded, bundled, offline sample sets (Grand, Upright, Electric) played through per-note sample voices, plus three honestly synthesized types (Clav, Digital, Misc) and the Phase 1 live-synthesis voice as a labelled fallback. Every sound reaches one AudioContext through per-layer buses, the ordered effect units, the shared Rotary, a master gain, and a limiter. Recorded audio is never generated, and generated audio is never described as a recording.
Generated sound sources
  • Phase 1 additive piano voice (live synthesis) — Six sine partials per note with a small inharmonic stretch, velocity-dependent partial levels and low-pass cutoff, and exponential decay. Generated at note-on. Used as the labelled fallback for Grand, Upright, and Electric while their sets load or after they fail. Not a recording.
  • Clav (synthesized) — Sawtooth plus square oscillators through a decaying low-pass filter with a short decay and quick release. An approximation of clavinet character, not a model of the Clavinet D6 pickups (manual p. 24).
  • Digital (synthesized FM) — Two-operator FM: a sine carrier with a sine modulator whose index decays after the strike, giving a bell-like upper spectrum. A stand-in for digital piano character.
  • Marimba (synthesized) — Three inharmonic sine modes at ratios 1, 4, and 10 with fast decay on the upper two.
  • Simulated sympathetic string resonance — When a note is struck with String Res on and other keys are held, each held note rings at its fundamental at a low level for about 3 seconds. A simulation of the effect described on manual p. 25; no pedal-down samples are used.
  • Generated reverb impulse responses — Six room impulse responses generated at run time from a fixed-seed noise generator with per-type decay (Booth 0.22 s to Cathedral 4.5 s), early reflections, and spring echoes for Spring. Not recordings of real rooms.

Phase 3

Complete Stage 4 system

Audio strategy
Phase 3 completes the instrument on one AudioContext. The three recorded, bundled, offline piano sample sets (Grand, Upright, Electric) are unchanged from Phase 2. Every other sound is live synthesis, declared in generatedSources: the organ (four models built from sine partials, percussion, key click, vibrato and chorus), the synth (fourteen required waveforms, filters, envelopes, LFO, voice modes), and the Phase 2 piano synthesis and fallback. Generated buffers (the synth white-noise loop, the organ key-click burst, and the reverb impulses) are generated at run time and are never described as recordings. Every sound reaches the Phase 2 graph: organ and synth chains with their own six-unit effect chains, the shared Rotary, master gain, and limiter.
Generated sound sources
  • Phase 1 additive piano voice (live synthesis) — Six sine partials per note with a small inharmonic stretch, velocity-dependent partial levels and low-pass cutoff, and exponential decay. Generated at note-on. Used as the labelled fallback for Grand, Upright, and Electric while their sets load or after they fail. Not a recording.
  • Clav (synthesized) — Sawtooth plus square oscillators through a decaying low-pass filter with a short decay and quick release. An approximation of clavinet character, not a model of the Clavinet D6 pickups (manual p. 24).
  • Digital (synthesized FM) — Two-operator FM: a sine carrier with a sine modulator whose index decays after the strike, giving a bell-like upper spectrum. A stand-in for digital piano character.
  • Marimba (synthesized) — Three inharmonic sine modes at ratios 1, 4, and 10 with fast decay on the upper two.
  • Simulated sympathetic string resonance — When a note is struck with String Res on and other keys are held, each held note rings at its fundamental at a low level for about 3 seconds. A simulation of the effect described on manual p. 25; no pedal-down samples are used.
  • Generated reverb impulse responses — Six room impulse responses generated at run time from a fixed-seed noise generator with per-type decay (Booth 0.22 s to Cathedral 4.5 s), early reflections, and spring echoes for Spring. Not recordings of real rooms.
  • Organ tone engines (B3, Vox, Farf, Pipe 1, Pipe 2) — Sine partials per drawbar, one rule per model: B3 linear per drawbar on 9 footages; Vox flat over 7 partials with the ninth drawbar dulling the upper partials; Farf on/off registers with odd-weighted partials; Pipe 1 a 1/ratio rank series; Pipe 2 the Pipe 1 engine with a brighter 1/ratio^0.7 slope (reuses the Pipe 1 engine, as the spec allows). Generated at note-on. Not recordings.
  • B3 percussion (2nd or 3rd harmonic, single-triggered) — One sine partial at the second or third harmonic with a linear decay of 0.25 s (fast) or 0.9 s (slow), level by soft/normal and the volume knob. Retriggers only when the layer is silent (manual p. 20).
  • Key click (generated noise burst) — A 6 ms burst of deterministic noise (fixed-seed generator) at note-on, at a fixed low level. Generated, not a recording.
  • Vibrato and chorus (LFO detune) — A 6.1 Hz sine LFO drives the detune of every partial (V1 to V3, 10 to 35 cents at full depth). C1 to C3 mix a pitch-modulated copy of the partials with the dry set at 25 to 65 percent.
  • Synth source recipes (Pure, Sync, Multi, Super, FM-H) — Pure: sine, triangle, saw, and square oscillators, and pulse waves from Fourier coefficients (33 percent and 10 percent duty). Multi and Super: stacks of detuned saws or squares, spread by Osc Ctrl. Sync: a second oscillator at a relative pitch of 1 to 4 times, a documented approximation of hard sync (no oscillator reset). FM 2-op: a sine carrier with a 2x modulator whose depth is Osc Ctrl.
  • White noise (generated loop) — A two-second loop of deterministic white noise, generated once per context. Not a recording.
  • Synth filters, envelopes, and LFO — LP12, LP24 (two cascaded low-pass stages), HP, and BP biquad filters with keyboard tracking, resonance, and a soft-clip drive stage. Amplifier, filter, and oscillator envelopes built from scheduled ramps; a finite decay settles at 0.7 (amplifier) or 0.5 (filter and oscillator) of its peak, and a maximum decay holds at the peak. LFO: triangle, two saws, square, and a Sample and Hold shape, which is a fixed pseudo-random periodic wave (deterministic, not a stepped random sample).
  • Arpeggiator sequencer (note events, no audio source) — A deterministic sequencer that schedules note-on and note-off events on a fixed step grid from the clock. It has no audio of its own; its notes are played by the synth layers.

Cause: .row uses flex-wrap: nowrap with .section overflow: visible (artifact/src/styles.css:106,153-161), so rows wider than their section spill into the next band.
  2. major Drag gestures never end: all 52 continuous controls follow a released pointer: useContinuous returns a handler named onPointerEnd (artifact/src/components/Controls.tsx:75-83), spread onto each control at lines 109, 123, 138, 152 and 166. onPointerEnd is not a React DOM event name, so it is never bound and drag.current is never cleared on pointer up. Pressing Layer B focus moves the FX focus with it (effects.focus-b aria-pressed true, focus-a false), which is the spec's focusFollowsLayerFocus.
  • Group mode shares a chain: with Piano group pressed, turning Mod 1 on under focus B leaves it reading on under focus A as well.
  • Defect: both octave controls are unreachable by pointer. All 25 hit-test points on piano.layer-a-octave and piano.layer-b-octave resolve to other elements, and a real mouse click at their centres leaves aria-valuenow at 50. They can still be driven by keyboard (focus plus ArrowUp moved both to 52 percent), so the parameter works but the mouse cannot reach it.
Detailed piano controls 3/4 75
  • String Res measurably rings the held strings. Holding four notes from the computer keyboard, waiting 3.5 s for them to decay, then striking a fifth: with String Res on the post-strike window measures rms 0.00289 / peak 0.0180 / centroid 805 Hz / low-band 0.0008; with it off, 0.00216 / 0.0115 / 1078 Hz / 0.0004. Two repeats agreed (on 0.00299/0.0175/815, off 0.00203/0.0117/1082).
  • KB Touch and Dyn Comp move level in the documented direction: KB Touch Heavy peak 0.00847, Medium 0.01008, Light 0.01849; Dyn Comp Off 0.00953, 1 0.00986, 2 0.01931, 3 0.02007 - monotonic, matching 'raises the level of softer strokes'.
  • Timbre and Unison change the spectrum: Timbre knob 0 / 60 / 100 percent gives centroid 821 / 877 / 1100 Hz; Unison Off / 1 / 2 / 3 gives 827 / 856 / 883 / 870 Hz with rms 0.00358 / 0.00281 / 0.00317 / 0.00287 as the detuned copies spread.
  • Soft Release lengthens the release: with it on, the normalised release-window energy measures t20-40 0.071 and t40-70 0.008; with it off, 0.008 and 0.000.
  • Shortfalls: Timbre is a percent knob whose readout never names the step (it reads '60 percent', while src/audio/timbre.ts and src/model/audioSettings.ts do map it onto the spec's Off/Soft/Mid/Bright steps), and Unison's centre is covered by program.button-1 overflowing out of the Program band, so it only responds to clicks on 9 of its 25 hit-test points.
Input reliability & failure behaviour 4/4 100
  • Pointer and computer keyboard both reach the full lifecycle: a 12-note cluster pressed from the keyboard rendered peak 0.111 with no clipping and no stuck notes, returning to exactly 0.0 rms after release.
  • A real focus change (opening and fronting a second page while a key is held) releases the voices: rms falls from 0.00703 while sounding to 0.00057 afterwards.
  • Failure degrades visibly, not silently: with every request to **/samples/** aborted, the piano still plays (peak 0.0196) and the status reads 'Grand: grand samples failed to load (Failed to fetch). Playing the labelled live-synthesis fallback, not the recorded set.' - it never claims the recorded library is ready, and no page error is thrown.
  • MIDI is reported honestly when unavailable: 'MIDI access denied. Keyboard and pointer still play the piano.' Device-level CC64 could not be exercised in this headless environment; the artifact covers it in src/input/midi.test.ts.
  • Across every interaction pass in this evaluation - six type changes, all effect units and types, layer and focus changes, pedal routes, the asset-failure run and the narrow-viewport run - the page logged zero page errors and zero console errors.
