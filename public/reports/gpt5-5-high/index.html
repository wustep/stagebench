What worked

  • Decorative-control boundaryPanel controls update only local presentation state in controlState; they are not wired to the piano engine or any system state.
  • Silhouette, chassis, and geometryThe rendered instrument uses the Stage 4 73 aspect ratio 3.0951 and the sealed desktop capture shows one continuous red chassis with rails, front lip, end cheeks, and no marketing hero.
  • Complete hardware inventory and placementThe artifact defines six sections in the documented order and fractions: performance, organ, piano, program, synth, and effects.
  • Materials, color, typography, and displaysScreenshots show credible red chassis material, dark inset panels, white/black key material, green LED ladders, and blue OLED treatments.

Priority issues

  1. Narrow 390px rendering preserves the whole instrument but compresses it to about 121px tall, making detailed controls and labels mostly illegible.
  2. Visual inventory is dense and sectionally correct, but many hardware details are simplified into schematic generic controls rather than reference-accurate labels, LEDs, and exact placements.
  3. Audio verification relies on stateful/null-adapter tests; it does not measure actual rendered Web Audio output or node cleanup at the browser boundary.
  4. GeneratedPianoEngine keeps released voices in its internal snapshot until cleanupReleased or later polyphony stealing; the app does not schedule cleanupReleased.
  5. The UI exposes a ready generated-synthesis status but does not exercise loading, error, or playable fallback states.
